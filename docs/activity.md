# Activity

Activity records what was in front of you, all day and during focus sessions. It answers events (an app comes forward, the screen locks, the Mac sleeps) and otherwise wakes only for the next thing that could change, at most every four seconds to read the front tab or document title. It does not infer productivity or read document contents. Session recaps are available from the finish card, the Activity session list and the day ribbon.

## All day

`ActivityRecorder` is owned by `FocusController` and runs apart from the session engine. A stretch closes when the app, site or title changes, or at idle, lock or sleep. Past the idle cutoff (5 minutes without input by default; adjustable) time stops at the last input and starts again when you come back. Inside a running focus session reading in silence still counts, as it always has.

One JSON file a day lives at `~/Library/Application Support/Nidus/Activity/YYYY-MM-DD.json`: totals, and an ordered, bounded timeline. Each stretch holds the app, the site, the page or window title and, for a web page, its full address. Websites are counted under their host without a leading `www.` (`nature.com`, `docs.google.com`, `scholar.google.com`), with service labels (Google Docs, Claude, Word, Claude Science, Capsid) ahead of a domain. Clicking a row in Websites or Documents opens its most recent address, or for a document without one, brings up its app. Export activity writes one row a stretch (date, start, end, app, host, title, URL, in-focus flag), cut where a session starts or ends; sessions without a daily record give their own rows. Files are written off the main thread. Daily records are kept 90 days by default (30 days, 1 year or forever), pruned at launch and daily.

Pausing for an hour from the menu bar stops all recording. Turning tracking off stops it at once and discards the open stretch; saved days stay. Excluding an app stops recording it, removes it from the running session, and hides it from Activity and exports; its saved time stays on disk and comes back if the app is let in again. Clear history removes sessions and every day file.

## Views

The page opens on Today, with the day drawn to clock scale: stretches colored by series, empty track for time away or idle, and focus sessions as clay bars above that open their recap. It is colored by app, like the Apps list; hovering names the site or document. With all-day on and no session running, the idle popover reads "Today 5h 12m · mostly Word". Today, Week, and Month offer previous/next navigation and project filters. One ranked list switches between Apps, Websites and Documents; Documents lists page titles and document names. "During focus only" narrows any view to the sessions' start and end, so a pause inside a session is included. A day with a daily record is counted from it, and sessions without one from their own record, so no seconds count twice. On Week and Month the daily chart stacks exclusive app/service categories (top four and Other); selecting a day filters session recaps. Daily totals group whole sessions by their start day. New sessions also retain a bounded ordered timeline for their recap. Focused time and observed time are separate; gaps remain unobserved. App, recognized-site and title lists describe different views of the same seconds and must not be added together.

App and title rankings show seconds below one minute and readable minute/hour labels for longer durations. A title is grouped with its app/service to prevent accidental cross-app merging. Different documents with the same name inside the same app/service can combine. Titles are not stable document identifiers and cannot be used to reopen documents. Earlier app-only history remains readable; detailed titles cannot be reconstructed retrospectively.

New recaps show observed intervals, known document switches, context switches, and the longest observed same-context stretch. Pauses, sleep, relaunch, excluded apps and skipped samples break continuity. The timeline retains at most 512 segments; when capped, the UI explicitly labels metrics as applying to retained observations. Older sessions have no invented timeline or retrospective switch metrics. Project insight cards summarize observed time and leading apps/services by the session's assigned Nidus project.

- **Day by category.** Colored by category, the day's ribbon joins runs of one category and folds short interruptions into what they interrupted. What a block folded in stays as thin strips along its bottom edge, in that category's color, at most twelve a block. Hovering a block names what's inside it, the time actually counted there, and what it folded in.
- **Week at a glance.** A stacked bar per day by category for the seven days ending on the shown day, with each category's total against the seven days before. It reads from the same per-day digests as the hour grid. Clicking a day opens it.
- **Hour by hour.** Weekdays by hours, over the last four weeks from Today or the selected range from Week and Month. An hour takes the color of whatever filled most of it: Work, a category, or everything else. Finished days are worked out once, off the main thread, and kept until the window closes. Clicking an hour lists its top apps and websites across the range; that reads only the files for that weekday, off the main thread, and is let go when the hour is cleared.
- **Drift.** The top five chains in the shown range: three minutes or more on something, then, within two minutes and with nothing breaking the record between them, three minutes or more in a distracting category, as "Chrome → x.com, 3 times, 25 min". A chain out of Work comes first; one out of Uncategorized time still counts. A chain stays inside one day's file, and a day that dropped its start has none. Session recaps leave chains out: distractions are blocked there.
- **Inside an app.** In the Apps list, a row with anything named inside it opens in place: a browser to its sites and their pages, a document app to its files.

## Tracking and access

Activity, all-day tracking and titles are on by default; **Track activity** and **Track all day** under Tracking are the off switches. Record sessions must also be on. Titles need Accessibility access and websites need Automation access per browser, each asked for from a button there. Nothing leaves this Mac.

Browser recognition uses only the running foreground browser's selected tab. Supported browser profiles are Safari, Chrome, Opera Air, Brave and Chromium; Automation permission is requested only after a button click. Private and incognito windows are recorded like any other. Recognized services are Google Docs document pages, Claude, supported Word web hosts, and the local Claude Science interface. Local Science recognition requires the running native launcher, HTTP loopback port 8765, an expected project/task route (or home), and the exact generic selected-tab title `Claude Science`. This generic application label only identifies the service; it is never stored as a conversation name. The selected URL is checked again after the title read to reject a tab-switch mismatch. A session's own record keeps domains and titles but not addresses.

Document apps (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Pages, Numbers, Keynote, TextEdit, Preview, Acrobat, Claude Desktop and Claude Science) use the Accessibility title of the focused window, when access is available; Preview and Acrobat have their page numbers and own name taken off. Other apps count as app time, with no title. Only the title attribute is read; no content tree, screenshot, or keystrokes are recorded. Generic shell/dialog titles are ignored. An application that does not expose a meaningful title falls back to app time. Names can lag window/tab changes by a few seconds: probes run at most once every four seconds, one at a time, and cached detail expires after five seconds.

Disabling history or activity clears captured activity from the active checkpoint. Adding exclusions removes those apps from the active checkpoint. Clear history also clears current activity so removed samples cannot reappear at session end. JSON export includes the optional activity data; the old session CSV schema stays stable, and Export activity creates a separate detail CSV with spreadsheet-formula escaping.

## Capsid

Capsid (`local.sam.bench`) appears as its own app and has a separate, default-off “Share the task I’m viewing with Nidus” setting. With sharing enabled in Capsid, the active Capsid project/task label can be recorded. No shell-title guessing is used.

Capsid writes `~/Library/Application Support/Capsid/nidus-context.json` only for its visible, focused main window on a matching task route. The small version-1 file contains its PID, a Unix timestamp, project name and task name. The file is owner-readable/writable, refreshed every five seconds, and removed on focus loss, ineligible pages, disabling sharing, or quit. Nidus accepts at most 8 KiB, requires the foreground PID to match, and rejects timestamps older than 15 seconds or more than two seconds in the future. It combines the bounded project/task names for display. No conversation content, URL, network call or new OS permission is required. The Capsid setting and app exclusions still apply.

Claude Science's separate Safari interface is recognized as a service but exposes a static tab title, so its conversation names remain unavailable there. Opening the same task through Capsid enables the explicit metadata integration. Capsid remains selectable in Apps to open and tracking exclusions.

The existing Capsid integration uses Nidus's local `focus.json` and `local.sam.nidus.focus` notification. The optional `away: true` field now covers system lock/sleep pauses, keeping Capsid's Finished cards held. A manual pause, session end, break or quit releases focus normally. No activity, document name or goal is included in that status file. The new metadata file is separate from this existing focus-status contract.

## Validation / previews

`--render-activity-surfaces <directory>` renders a small set of isolated light/dark Activity views with explicitly fictional data. `--demo activity-filled --open activity` is an isolated interactive demo. Neither can probe real apps or use real history. Keep builds at two workers (`NIDUS_BUILD_JOBS`, default 2).

See `activity-claude-handoff.md` for final validation, installed build identity, and remaining live checks.
